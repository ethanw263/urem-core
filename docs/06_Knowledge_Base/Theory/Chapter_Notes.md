Axiomatic Decision — Recognition Systems Require Place Domain, Recognition Traces, and Transformation Structure:
A spatial recognition system is provisionally defined as a mechanism-bearing structure acting on an admissible place space. It must define a place domain, admit realized recognition traces, and contain or imply mechanisms by which latent spatial recognizability may become realized spatial recognition. The system need not assume perfect recognition, fixed recognition mass, single scale, single audience, or complete observability.

Section 4.2 — Independence of Recognition System Axioms
Axiomatic Decision — Recognition System Axioms Are Independent:
The three proposed spatial recognition system axioms — place domain, recognition traces, and transformation structure — are independent. A place domain does not imply recognition traces or transformation mechanisms; recognition traces do not imply a coherent place domain or transformation explanation; and transformation structure does not guarantee observable traces. Therefore all three are required for an admissible spatial recognition system in SRS.

Axiomatic Decision — Latent Spatial Recognizability Is a Relational Capacity:
Latent spatial recognizability should not be identified with suitability, intrinsic quality, expected recognition, or future popularity. Instead, it is provisionally defined as the latent capacity of a place to generate realized recognition under the mechanisms of a specified spatial recognition system.
