Preface

This document develops the mathematical foundations of Recognition Disequilibrium Theory.

The central question is not:

Where are favorable places?

Nor:

Which observations are anomalous?

The central question is:

How does a recognition system transform latent recognition potential into realized recognition, and where does that transformation fail?

Recognition Disequilibrium Theory studies the mismatch between what a recognition system could recognize and what it actually recognizes.

The purpose of this monograph is to define the primitive objects, axioms, derived structures, and mathematical machinery needed to study that mismatch rigorously.

Part I — Compressed Foundations
Chapter 1 — The Scientific Question

Recognition is not merely a score assigned to an object.

Recognition is a system-conditioned realization.

A place, object, idea, institution, region, or entity may possess latent recognition-generating capacity without that capacity being realized.

This creates the possibility of disequilibrium.

RDE begins from the claim that recognition failure is not always noise, nor merely prediction error. In some systems, unrealized recognition capacity may persist because of allocation constraints, transmission failure, shadowing, reinforcement, historical inertia, or system geometry.

The scientific object of RDE is therefore:

recognition realization failure

within a structured recognition system.
