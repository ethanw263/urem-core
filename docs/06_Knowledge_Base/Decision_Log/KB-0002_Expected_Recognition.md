# KB-0002 — Expected Recognition

**Status:** Accepted

**Date:** 2026

---

# Problem

Early versions of the framework measured physical exceptionality and observed recognition independently.

While this identified places with high physical quality and low recognition, it did not establish whether that level of recognition was actually unusual.

For example:

- A remote island may naturally have low recognition.
- A spectacular coastal viewpoint near a major highway may reasonably be expected to have high recognition.

Observed recognition alone cannot distinguish between these situations.

The framework therefore lacked an objective baseline against which recognition could be evaluated.

---

# Decision

Introduce an **Expected Recognition** model.

Expected Recognition estimates how much recognition a location should receive based on measurable characteristics rather than relying solely on observed popularity.

Recognition Disequilibrium is then defined as the difference between:

- Expected Recognition
- Observed Recognition

rather than between physical quality and observed recognition.

---

# Rationale

This decision transformed the framework from a descriptive mapping exercise into a predictive scientific model.

Instead of asking:

> "Which places have low recognition?"

the framework now asks:

> "Which places receive substantially less recognition than would be expected given their measurable characteristics?"

This distinction is fundamental.

Expected Recognition provides the statistical reference point required for meaningful comparison.

Without an expected value, under-recognition cannot be quantified objectively.

---

# Scientific Advantages

Introducing Expected Recognition provides several important benefits.

It allows the framework to:

- distinguish true under-recognition from naturally low-recognition locations
- compare locations with different geographic contexts
- generate falsifiable hypotheses
- support statistical validation
- enable counterfactual comparisons
- improve generalization across study regions

Expected Recognition also separates environmental characteristics from social recognition processes, allowing the framework to investigate why recognition diverges from expectation.

---

# Mathematical Significance

Expected Recognition became one of the central mathematical quantities within the framework.

Rather than treating recognition as an observed attribute alone, the framework models recognition as a variable that can be estimated, compared, and tested.

This opened the possibility for future work involving:

- probabilistic recognition models
- uncertainty estimation
- confidence intervals
- Bayesian formulations
- machine learning approaches
- causal inference
- recognition-flow dynamics

---

# Consequences

This decision fundamentally changed the interpretation of Recognition Disequilibrium.

The project no longer evaluates locations solely on physical quality.

Instead, it evaluates the discrepancy between expected and observed recognition.

Nearly every subsequent component of the framework—including validation, mechanism discovery, and theoretical development—depends upon this distinction.

---

# Long-Term Impact

Expected Recognition represents one of the core theoretical innovations of the RDE framework.

It establishes the statistical baseline required for measuring Recognition Disequilibrium and provides the foundation for future mathematical refinement, algorithm development, and cross-domain applications.
